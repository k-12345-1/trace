The brief

The wireframe,
reduced to a mark.

Two new candidates take the wireframe graphic as their basis, and they are the first here built from curves. That is a deliberate reversal of the straight-lines-only note: the reference form is a swept volume, and it cannot be made from mitred segments without becoming something else. The rest of the set stays angular so both directions can be judged side by side. White on vermilion throughout, and every mark is shown at 28 pixels, because that is where marks die.

Kaya

Monoline, held inside a square frame. The frame does the containing so the line inside can be loose.

Strava

One form repeated at two weights. The tint is what creates depth, not a gradient.

Black Diamond

Solid silhouette, and the notch of negative space is the whole idea. Survives at any size.

The rules they are built to

What makes a mark survive

Two weights, one colour

Where a mark has a secondary element it is the same white at reduced opacity, the way Strava tints its lower chevron. Never a second hue.

No hairlines

Nothing thinner than 6 percent of the tile. A 2px stroke looks elegant at 1024 and disappears at 28.

Inside the safe area

The mark sits within the middle 76 percent. iOS rounds the corners and the eye crops further.

Constructed, not drawn

Dome and Boulder are curved, but every curve is symmetric and generated rather than sketched. That is the difference between the reference graphic and the squiggle the app currently ships.

Frame has been dropped to make room, since it was the most derivative of the set. Apex went earlier: A peak with a dot sits in the most crowded corner of outdoor branding, beside Strava and every trail logo there is, and it was the least constructed of the five. Step replaces it. The mark currently shipping in the app is still none of these: an organic curved line on a dark ground, illustrative rather than built.
