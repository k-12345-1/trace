Visual identity · v0.1

The position

An instrument,
not a feed.

Every climbing app on the shelf is built like social media: saturated blue, full pills, Inter, a scrolling wall of other people's sends. This one watches how you move and tells you one true thing. It should look like a measuring device made by an outdoor company, which is exactly the space Arc'teryx and Kawa Club already occupy and no climbing app has taken.

The gap is real. Every climbing app clusters at the loud end. Every outdoor brand worth borrowing from sits at the quiet end. Nobody has brought technical-apparel restraint into a climbing app, and a training tool is exactly the product that earns it.

Colour

Oxblood and vermilion

Dark is not a preference, it is the product. The hero surface is always footage of you climbing in a dim gym, so the interface has to disappear behind it. Light exists for the other half of the app: session reports and trends, which are documents and want paper.

Where this comes from. Read off an Arc'teryx knit beanie: the body around #5C1A23, the Archaeopteryx around #E8452A. It solves the overlay problem the way the previous palette did, because vermilion is high in luminance and uncommon on plastic holds, and it avoids green altogether, which is the worst possible overlay hue in a gym full of green holds and green tape.

Why the ground is not the beanie. A fully saturated wine ground would cast every frame of climbing footage and fight it, and the hero surface here is always video. So the ground is that wine taken almost to black, and the wine itself is kept for raised surfaces where it can actually be seen: the focus banner, the washes, the app icon.

One thing to weigh. This pairing is strongly associated with Arc'teryx, who sell climbing gear. Colour is not protectable, but a climbing app in their palette can read as borrowing an identity rather than having one.

Overlay

Drawing on top of a wall of every colour

This is the one design problem no other app in the category has to solve. Climbing holds come in every saturated hue there is, so an overlay cannot rely on hue to stand out. It has to rely on luminance and outline.

Skeleton

Chalk, double-stroked

2px --chalk over a 5.5px near-black stroke at 75%. The dark halo is what makes it legible over a yellow hold and a black mat alike. It is never the accent colour: the skeleton is context, not a finding.

Centre-of-mass path

The only orange line

2px --accent with a soft glow, plus a 42% scrim over the footage underneath. The accent is spent here and almost nowhere else, because this trace is the product's headline measurement.

Leak markers

Rings, never fills

A 1.5px ring around the joint at fault, sized to the finding. A filled marker would hide the very thing it is pointing at.

Confidence

Say nothing when unsure

Below the tracking threshold the overlay does not draw and the app admits it could not see clearly. Confidently wrong coaching is the failure mode that kills the product.

Type

Archivo and IBM Plex Mono

Two families, three jobs. Archivo is a sturdy American grotesk that holds up in heavy weights and in tracked-out uppercase, which is the Arc'teryx move. Plex Mono does every number, because a metric should read like an instrument and not like body copy. In the iOS build these map to SF Pro and SF Mono.

Rules

What keeps it honest

Always

Hairlines before boxes

Separate things with a 1px rule and space. Reach for a bordered card only when something genuinely lifts off the page.

Always

Numbers in mono

Every metric, timestamp, angle and grade is tabular Plex Mono. It is what makes the app read as a measuring device.

Always

Units on readouts

Nats, degrees, seconds, multiples. A bare number is a score; a number with a unit is a measurement.

Never

More than one orange thing

Per screen. If the COM path is on screen, nothing else is accent coloured.

Never

Celebratory tone

No confetti, no streaks, no "crushing it". A training partner is specific and brief, and the app is not a game.

Never

Grade as the headline

The app has opinions about movement, not difficulty. Grades are a small mono label, never the biggest thing on a screen.

Note

The app is called Spotter. In bouldering your spotter is the person who watches you climb, which is exactly what the product is, and it carries the training-partner framing better than a physics term would. The working build ships under this name.
