Spotter is a native iOS app that watches you climb and tells you one thing to do better. This page is a faithful reproduction of its screens so it can be looked at without Xcode. The middle one plays.

Read this first

This is a mock, not the app. It is HTML redrawing the iOS interface. Nothing here records, tracks a pose, or analyses anything.

The climber is synthetic and so is the wall. No real person has been filmed. A generator draws an invented climber, and the app's real analysis code then runs over those joint positions.

The numbers are genuine output. Every entropy figure, elbow angle, finding and sequence grouping on this page was produced by the same Swift that ships in the app, exported straight into this page. They describe a climber who does not exist, computed honestly.

Why the overlay is chalk and orange

Climbing holds come in every saturated colour there is, so an overlay cannot rely on hue to stand out. The skeleton is chalk over a dark halo, which reads over a yellow hold and a black mat alike. The accent is spent only on the centre-of-mass trace, because that trace is the headline measurement.

Why it never names a hold

Everyone's beta is different, so Spotter measures how well you executed the sequence you chose rather than which sequence you chose. On the middle screen it has grouped three attempts into two sequences purely by the shape of the centre-of-mass path, and marked which of them cost less. It has no idea what is on the wall.

What it does when it cannot see

One climb in the list is marked low tracking. Below 55 percent confidence the app draws nothing and says nothing, because confidently wrong coaching is the failure mode that would kill the product.
