Photograph a wall

Then tap one hold. Every blob on the wall within a colour distance of it is picked out, so a route is read by its colour rather than from a database. It works on the first photo in any gym, and it cannot tell your route from unrelated holds of the same colour, which is why you drop the wrong ones.

How it works

Trace is a native iOS app written in Swift. A browser cannot run that, so this page is a port of the two things the app does, using the same arithmetic on the same thresholds.

Watching you climb

A body-pose model gives the joints in each frame. Everything after that is arithmetic: no model is trained, and nothing is uploaded. The centre of mass is a weighted sum of eleven body segments. Elbow angle is measured only while you are still, where "still" is relative to your own torso length so that a climber filmed from further away is not called still twice as often. Pauses, foot resets and the wander between moves come off the same series.

Where the app and this page differ: iOS uses Apple's Vision body-pose model, and this page uses MoveNet, which is the closest thing a browser can run. The joints are the same, the confidence numbers are not, so a clip may track a little better or worse here.

Reading a wall

Colours are compared in CIE Lab, where distance roughly matches what the eye calls a different colour, rather than in RGB where it does not. The holds of a route are the connected blobs within that distance, outlined by the convex hull of their pixels.

What is not here

The figure that climbs the route for you, the start and finish sticker reading, grades, gyms, and your library of past attempts are all in the iOS app and not on this page. Those lean on the parts of the app that hold state and read text, and this is meant to be the part you can try in one click.
