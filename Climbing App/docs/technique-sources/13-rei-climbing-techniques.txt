REI: Climbing techniques and moves (extracted)
Feet: feet are the foundation; step up, don't pull up. Edging (inside edge/big toe for stability on small holds, outside edge depending on direction) and smearing (friction, flatten the angle). Keep feet directly below you; look for footholds more than handholds; once set, keep the foot still; keep the heel low (high heel = less rubber, levers the foot off).
Balance: counter a side pull by pressing a foot the opposite way, pulling the opposite way with the other hand or a hooked foot, or leaning over hard as counterweight; otherwise you barn-door.
Efficiency: "straight arms are happy arms", skeleton takes the weight, even a slight bend means muscles are working. Hips: squared to the wall pushes weight away; keep one hip against the wall so weight is over the feet and you can lean back on straight arms; a close hip brings the shoulder close and changes the pull angle on handholds. Climb with your eyes: look for rests; use them, shake out.
Moves: back step (outside edge, hip to wall, easier to straighten arms), drop knee (extreme back step, foothold near hip level, knee points down, great for reach on steep walls), stemming (counter-pressure, big leg muscles), flagging (counter-balance when holds are all on one side), layback (straight arms, feet do the work, heels low if smearing), mantle, undercling (find high footholds, hold at waist level, arms straighter), side pull (balance the sideways pull), gaston (push out, elbow out, force from the shoulder), palming.
