Gripped: A comprehensive guide to climbing technique (extracted claims)
- Good technique = efficient movement adapted to the individual, not one universal standard.
- Hips: "keeping the hips open" is fundamental; bring the groin toward the wall in all scenarios except drop-knees. Open hips let weight go through the feet and reduce hand strain.
- Arms: straight arms are more efficient than perpetual lock-off; "keep your arms straight until you need to pull"; hands as pivot points from which to generate.
- Weight: "keep the feet on, and your weight under you as much as possible"; static climbing = pushing a portion of weight through the feet.
- Footwork types: toeing-in (needs core), smear, edging (stiff shoes), heel hook (drops difficulty a lot), toe hook (core), heel-toe cam (can injure if stuck in a fall), toe-jam, knee-bar (hands rest if bomber), bicycle, reverse-bicycle.
- Static positions: climbing square (removes subtlety, not efficient); flag with hip open pressing into the wall with the inside edge; back-flag; inside-flag (rarely forced); perching (pistol-squat); drop knee rotates hips perpendicular to the wall, the only time hip openness should be compromised.
- Dynamic: momentum should come from the hips, not pulling. Moon-kick (foot as a whip), hip generation (lead with the hips), the pop (lock-off then slight momentum). Dissipation: zero-momentum move (land in equilibrium), post-catch flick / scorpion kick (let momentum flow through), catching a lock-off high reduces swing.
- Faults: static-only climbers catching holds with full momentum need much more strength; flagging without pressing into the wall; resisting momentum gets you carried off; refusing static or dynamic style halves your technical skill.
- Efficiency = constant movement, alternating static and dynamic.
