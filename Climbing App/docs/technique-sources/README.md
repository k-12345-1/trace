# Technique sources

Reference text behind Trace's findings, advice and drills, checked 29 September 2026.
Files 1 to 10 are Neil Gresham's UKClimbing technique series (with Nina Tappin,
Steve Golley, Gill Peet); 11 is Gripped's comprehensive guide; 12 is 99boulders on
holds and moves; 13 is REI's climbing techniques page. Plain text captured from the
live pages so the claims Trace makes can be traced to a sentence.

Where Trace and the sources meet:

- Bent arms while static: Gresham, Posture, tip 1 and 2. Default hang is arms
  virtually straight with a slight bend and shoulders engaged; do not sag onto
  straight arms; do not hold lock-offs for more than a few seconds.
- Weight hanging off your arms: Gresham, General approach ("arms straight and hips
  twisted in"); REI (one hip against the wall, weight over the feet); Gripped (hips
  open, groin toward the wall except in a drop-knee).
- Imprecise feet: Gresham, Improve Your Movement, drill 1: silent, no scuff, first
  time, no test, eye on the foot until it is placed.
- Reading the route while hanging: Gresham, General approach, pointer 4: plan from the
  floor, mime the hand sequence.
- Start-stop movement: Gresham, Improve Your Movement, drill 3: flow through fixed
  positions rather than stopping on them.
- Hanging outside your contacts: REI balance tactics; Gripped on flagging without
  pressing into the wall.
- Catching outside the deadpoint: 99boulders deadpoint; Gripped on momentum from the
  hips and the zero-momentum move.

Added 29 September 2026, in `TechniqueEngine`, because the sources name them and
the joints show them:

- Reaching before stepping: Gresham, General approach ("over-reaching for
  handholds instead of moving our feet up first"). An upward reach the arm made
  with both feet planted for the second and a half before it.
- Square hips on the reach: Gresham ("hips parallel and arms bent"); REI ("keep
  one hip pushed up against the wall"); Gripped (groin toward the wall). Hips at
  their widest at launch and the elbow bent at the catch, on the same reach.
- Holding a lock-off: Gresham, Posture, tip 2 ("minimize holding lock-offs for
  longer than a few seconds"). A bent arm held still on its hold past 2.5 s.
- Elbows flared: Gresham, Posture, tip 3 (chicken-winging). An elbow above its
  shoulder with the hand below it, on a hand that is not moving.
- Stepping too high: Gresham, Use of the toe ("several small steps are nearly
  always preferable to one big one"). A foot landing within a third of a torso
  of hip height.

Each needs a pattern across the climb, not one event, because none can see the
wall: a rock-over is a high step on purpose and a gaston puts the elbow out.

What the sources cover that Trace cannot measure with a 15-joint pose, and does
not pretend to: grip and hold types (Handholds 1 to 3, Undercuts and sidepulls),
heel and toe hooks, which part of the shoe is on the hold, pockets, smearing
against edging, keeping the heel low, relaxed grip, breathing, and looking at
the foot as it lands (the model has a nose but no gaze). Momentum from the hips
on a dynamic move and pacing are partly covered by the deadpoint, start-stop and
hesitation findings and not measured on their own.
